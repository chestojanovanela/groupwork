RESEARCH NOTES

What are we testing?

StudyLab asks whether immediate cued recall differs after information is presented as written pairs, picture-supported pairs, or spoken pairs. Each session uses 18 preselected pairs, assigns six to each format, and randomizes the block order and list-to-format pairing.

Key limitation: preferences for visual, auditory, or written presentation are not established diagnostic “learning styles.” A single session cannot show that matching someone's preference to a format improves learning. Small item sets, short retention intervals, audio quality, and the extra cues in the picture condition all limit interpretation.

Audio uses your browser's speech synthesis. It may sound different across devices; if unavailable, the app records a text fallback. The results are exploratory.
